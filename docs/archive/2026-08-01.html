🧠 Data Science Topic of the Day

🎓 Transformer Embeddings & Semantic Search

Turning words and entities into geometry — so 'similar' becomes findable at scale.

What It Is

Transformer embeddings convert text (or structured records) into dense numeric vectors in a high-dimensional space, where semantic similarity maps to geometric proximity. Unlike keyword search — which matches exact tokens — embedding-based semantic search finds records that mean the same thing even when they use different words. Models like BERT, Sentence-BERT (SBERT), and OpenAI's text-embedding series are the workhorses. Each piece of text is passed through a transformer's encoder layers; the resulting vector (typically 384–1536 dimensions) is a learned, compressed representation of meaning. At D&B, this is the technology that can bridge the gap between messy, real-world company name variations ('IBM Corp', 'International Business Machines', 'IBM Deutschland GmbH') and the canonical DUNS-anchored entity — making entity resolution smarter, faster, and language-agnostic.

How It Works

1. TOKENISATION: Input text is split into subword tokens (e.g. 'Salesforce' → ['Sale', '##sfor', '##ce']). Each token maps to a learned embedding. 2. POSITIONAL ENCODING: Because transformers have no inherent sequence order, positional signals are added to each token embedding so the model understands word order. 3. SELF-ATTENTION (the core): Every token attends to every other token, computing weighted relevance scores. This lets 'bank' in 'river bank' pull different context than 'bank' in 'central bank'. Multi-head attention runs this in parallel across multiple subspaces, capturing different relationship types simultaneously. 4. POOLING TO SENTENCE VECTOR: The token-level outputs are pooled (mean pooling is standard in SBERT) into a single fixed-length vector representing the whole input. 5. SIMILARITY SEARCH: At query time, the query is embedded into the same space. Cosine similarity (or dot product) between the query vector and all indexed vectors ranks results by semantic closeness. In production, Approximate Nearest Neighbour (ANN) libraries — FAISS, ScaNN, HNSW — make this sub-millisecond even across hundreds of millions of vectors. 6. FINE-TUNING (optional but powerful): Pre-trained embeddings can be fine-tuned on domain-specific pairs using contrastive loss (e.g. SimCSE, triplet loss) — teach the model that 'Acme Corp Ltd' and 'Acme Corporation' are close, while 'Acme Corp' and 'Ajax Holdings' are far. D&B's labelled DUNS match/no-match pairs are exactly the supervision signal needed here.

When To Use

— ENTITY RESOLUTION at scale: fuzzy company name matching across 600M+ records where rules-based approaches break down on multilingual, abbreviated, or misspelled inputs. — SEMANTIC PROSPECTING: a sales rep types 'mid-size logistics company expanding into Southeast Asia' — embed the query, retrieve the closest matching D&B company profiles rather than forcing structured filter UI. — INTENT CLASSIFICATION in agentic workflows: embed an incoming CRM note or support ticket and classify it as churn signal, expansion opportunity, or neutral — feeding the Pipeline Risk or Account Expansion agent archetypes. — DUPLICATE DETECTION in data pipelines: embed company descriptions or addresses; clusters of near-identical vectors flag duplicate records before they pollute downstream models. — CROSS-LINGUAL SEARCH: multilingual embedding models (mBERT, LaBSE) embed company names in German, Japanese, or Portuguese into the same space as English queries — critical for D&B's global coverage.

🎯 Worked Example — D&B Context

SCENARIO — Intelligent Prospecting Agent using D&B Embeddings. PROBLEM: A D&B enterprise customer (a B2B SaaS vendor) wants to find 'companies similar to our top 10 accounts' from D&B's 600M record universe — without manually specifying SIC codes, revenue bands, or geographies. STEP 1 — PROFILE EMBEDDING: Take the D&B data profiles (business description, industry text, Hoovers summary, trade style) for the customer's top 10 accounts. Concatenate key text fields. Pass through a fine-tuned SBERT model to produce 10 query vectors. STEP 2 — CENTROID QUERY: Average the 10 vectors into a single 'ideal customer profile' centroid vector. STEP 3 — ANN SEARCH: Run FAISS index over the pre-embedded D&B company corpus (600M records, indexed offline nightly). Retrieve top-5,000 nearest neighbours by cosine similarity. STEP 4 — SIGNAL LAYERING: Re-rank the 5,000 candidates using structured D&B signals — Paydex score (payment reliability), Buydex (intent to purchase), employee growth rate, corporate family tree depth. Candidates with high semantic similarity AND strong Paydex AND positive Buydex signal float to the top. STEP 5 — EXPLAINABILITY: For each recommended prospect, surface the nearest 'anchor account' from the customer's existing portfolio plus the top contributing text features (via token-level attention weights). The sales rep sees: 'Similar to Acme Logistics — both show multi-modal distribution language, Southeast Asia expansion signals, and 200–500 employee band.' OUTCOME: The Prospecting Agent surfaces 150 high-fit, high-intent accounts the rule-based ICP filter would have missed — entirely new TAM white space.
