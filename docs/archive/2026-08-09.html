🧠 Data Science Topic of the Day

🎓 Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)

Give your LLM a memory it can trust — grounding AI answers in verified, up-to-date facts rather than stale training weights.

What It Is

RAG is an architecture that enhances a Large Language Model by pairing it with a live retrieval system. Instead of relying solely on patterns baked into model weights at training time, RAG dynamically fetches relevant documents or records at inference time and injects them into the LLM's context window before it generates a response. The result is an AI that can answer questions about proprietary, recent, or highly specific data — without the cost and risk of retraining or fine-tuning the base model. For D&B, this is the architectural backbone that makes 'AI Decision Intelligence' practical: the LLM provides reasoning and language fluency, while D&B's 600M+ business records provide the verified ground truth.

How It Works

1. INDEXING (offline): Source documents — financial records, firmographics, news, contracts — are chunked into passages and converted into dense vector embeddings using an encoder model (e.g. text-embedding-3-large). Those vectors are stored in a vector database (Pinecone, Weaviate, pgvector). Crucially, each chunk retains metadata: DUNS number, entity name, timestamp, source system. 2. RETRIEVAL (online, at query time): The user's query is embedded with the same encoder. A nearest-neighbour search returns the top-k most semantically similar chunks. A re-ranker (e.g. a cross-encoder model) optionally re-scores them for precision. 3. AUGMENTATION: Retrieved chunks are inserted into the LLM prompt as grounding context — 'Here is relevant information: [chunks]. Now answer the user's question.' 4. GENERATION: The LLM synthesises a response constrained by the retrieved evidence. Because the source chunks are surfaced, every claim is traceable — the 'Explainability First' principle in action. Key design choices that determine RAG quality: - Chunk size & overlap: too small loses context; too large dilutes signal. - Hybrid retrieval: combining dense (semantic) + sparse (BM25 keyword) search improves recall on exact identifiers like DUNS numbers or ticker symbols. - Metadata filtering: pre-filtering by entity type, geography, or freshness date before vector search dramatically sharpens relevance. - HyDE (Hypothetical Document Embeddings): generate a hypothetical ideal answer first, embed that, then retrieve — useful when queries are short and ambiguous. - Agentic RAG: rather than a single retrieval pass, a LangChain/LangGraph agent iteratively retrieves, checks sufficiency, and retrieves again — critical for multi-hop reasoning across corporate family trees.

When To Use

Use RAG when: (a) answers depend on data that changes faster than you can retrain a model — D&B Paydex scores, firmographic updates, news signals; (b) you need auditable, citable outputs — compliance, credit underwriting, enterprise sales; (c) your knowledge base is too large and heterogeneous to fine-tune on; (d) hallucination risk is unacceptable — RAG constrains the LLM to retrieved evidence. Do NOT rely on RAG alone when: complex multi-step numerical reasoning is needed (add a code-execution tool), or when the query requires knowledge truly absent from your corpus (RAG cannot fabricate what was never indexed).

🎯 Worked Example — D&B Context

SCENARIO — D&B Account Intelligence Agent for an enterprise sales rep: The rep types: 'What is the current financial health of Acme Corp and any subsidiaries in Germany, and is now a good time to expand the contract?' Step 1 — Entity Resolution first: the agent calls D&B's DUNS resolution API to anchor 'Acme Corp' to a canonical DUNS number and retrieve the full corporate family tree, identifying three German subsidiaries. Step 2 — Parallel RAG retrieval across four indexes: (i) D&B Paydex & Buydex scores for all four entities, filtered to last-90-days records; (ii) D&B news & risk event index — any CCJs, leadership changes, regulatory actions; (iii) internal CRM notes and support tickets; (iv) macro index — German manufacturing PMI, EUR/USD trend. Step 3 — Re-ranking: a cross-encoder scores the 40 retrieved chunks, surfacing the 8 most relevant. The German subsidiary shows a Paydex score drop from 78 to 61 over 60 days — a buying-window risk signal from the Temporal Analytics layer. Step 4 — Augmented generation: the LLM receives the 8 chunks plus the rep's question. It responds: 'Acme Corp DE GmbH shows payment deterioration (Paydex 78→61, 60 days). Recommend pausing expansion conversation and scheduling a risk review. Parent entity (US HQ) remains strong at Paydex 82. Full source citations: [chunk IDs].' Step 5 — Explainability: every sentence in the output links back to a retrievable chunk with a DUNS number, timestamp, and source system — audit-ready, not a black box. This is the Pipeline Risk & Revenue Protection agent archetype operating at its best — RAG is the mechanism that makes D&B's proprietary data the competitive moat inside any agentic platform.
