🧠 Data Science Topic of the Day

🎓 Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)

Give your AI a memory it can trust — grounding LLM answers in verified, up-to-date facts instead of hallucinated confidence.

What It Is

RAG is an architecture that combines a retrieval system (a search engine over your own documents or data) with a generative large language model. Instead of relying solely on what a model learned during training — which is frozen in time and often vague on proprietary facts — RAG dynamically fetches relevant context at query time and hands it to the LLM as grounding material. The model then generates an answer that is explicitly anchored to retrieved passages. The result: an AI that sounds like a knowledgeable expert on YOUR data, not just a confident guesser drawing on public internet training. For D&B, this is the bridge between a generic LLM and one that can speak authoritatively about a specific company's Paydex history, corporate family tree, or credit risk trajectory.

How It Works

RAG has five mechanical steps. (1) INDEXING: Your source documents — PDFs, database records, D&B company profiles, news articles — are chunked into small passages and converted into dense vector embeddings using an encoder model (e.g. text-embedding-3-large). These embeddings are stored in a vector database (Pinecone, Weaviate, pgvector). (2) RETRIEVAL: When a user submits a query, the same encoder converts it into a query vector. A nearest-neighbour search returns the top-k most semantically similar chunks from the index. (3) RE-RANKING (optional but important): A cross-encoder re-ranker (e.g. Cohere Rerank, ColBERT) scores the top-k candidates more precisely and re-orders them for relevance, filtering noise before the LLM sees anything. (4) AUGMENTATION: The top chunks are injected into the LLM prompt as a 'context' block. The prompt instructs the model: 'Answer ONLY using the provided context. If the answer is not in the context, say so.' (5) GENERATION: The LLM produces its response grounded in the retrieved evidence. Advanced variants — called Agentic RAG or RAG-as-a-tool — let an agent decide when to retrieve, from which index, and whether to retrieve multiple times in a chain before answering. Chunking strategy matters enormously: too small and you lose context; too large and you overwhelm the LLM and dilute relevance scores. Sentence-window chunking and hierarchical chunking (storing both a small retrieval chunk and its parent paragraph for generation) are current best practices. Evaluation metrics include Context Precision, Context Recall, Answer Faithfulness, and Answer Relevance — the RAGAS framework packages all four.

When To Use

Use RAG when: (a) your answers must be grounded in proprietary, frequently updated, or domain-specific data that was never in LLM training data; (b) hallucination is a business risk, not just an annoyance — credit decisions, compliance, contract language; (c) you need citations/provenance so users can verify the source; (d) fine-tuning is too expensive or too slow given how often your data changes. Do NOT use plain RAG when: reasoning chains are long and multi-hop (prefer Agentic RAG or graph RAG); structured numerical computation is needed (use tool-calling with a code interpreter instead); or the query requires synthesising hundreds of documents simultaneously (consider long-context models or map-reduce summarisation pipelines).

🎯 Worked Example — D&B Context

D&B Account Intelligence Agent — 'Why is this prospect risky right now?' A sales rep in Salesforce Agentforce types: 'Summarise the risk posture of Acme Manufacturing Inc. and flag anything material from the last 90 days.' Without RAG, a generic LLM invents plausible-sounding but unverifiable claims. With RAG: (1) The query is embedded and fired against two indexes simultaneously — D&B's structured entity store (Paydex scores, DBT derogatory trade, corporate family tree JSON for Acme's DUNS) and an unstructured news/filing index (recent 8-Ks, supplier news, court records ingested nightly). (2) Top-k retrieval surfaces: Paydex dropped from 72 to 54 over 60 days, three new derogatory payment experiences from key suppliers, and a news chunk noting the parent entity filed for creditor protection in Germany last week. (3) The LLM prompt is: 'Using ONLY the retrieved D&B records and news excerpts below, explain the risk posture of Acme Manufacturing Inc. Cite each claim with its source document ID.' (4) The agent responds with a structured narrative: 'Acme's Paydex score has deteriorated 25% in 60 days [Source: D&B Trade File, DUNS 123456789], driven by three new slow-pay experiences from Tier-1 suppliers [Source: D&B Trade Experiences Q3-2026]. The immediate parent entity, Acme GmbH, entered creditor protection proceedings on 14 Sep 2026 [Source: Reuters filing index]. Recommend flagging for Pipeline Risk review before contract renewal.' The rep gets a verifiable, cited, actionable answer in under three seconds — exactly D&B's 'Explainability First / Ground Truth' value proposition made real.
