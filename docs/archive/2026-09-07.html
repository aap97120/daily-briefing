🧠 Data Science Topic of the Day

🎓 Survival Analysis

How long until something happens — and what drives it.

What It Is

Survival analysis is a branch of statistics designed to model the time until an event of interest occurs — originally death in clinical trials, but in B2B analytics: churn, contract renewal, payment default, or deal close. Unlike standard regression, it explicitly handles 'censored' observations — customers still alive at the end of your observation window who haven't experienced the event yet. Throwing those records away or treating them as non-events both destroy information; survival analysis uses them correctly. The two workhorses are the Kaplan-Meier estimator (non-parametric survival curves) and the Cox Proportional Hazards model (semi-parametric regression that quantifies how covariates accelerate or decelerate the event). Modern extensions — DeepSurv, survival forests, and competing-risks models — bring the framework into the ML era.

How It Works

1. DEFINE THE CLOCK & EVENT: Choose a time zero (contract start, first invoice, DUNS first observed) and a crisp event definition (first 90-day delinquency, churn, upsell). 2. HANDLE CENSORING: Every entity that hasn't experienced the event by your cut-off date contributes partial information. The likelihood function splits cleanly: observed events contribute their density; censored observations contribute their survival probability up to that point. 3. KAPLAN-MEIER CURVES: Non-parametric step-function estimate of S(t) = P(T > t). No assumptions about the underlying distribution. Perfect for visualising and comparing cohorts (e.g. firms with Paydex < 50 vs. ≥ 80). 4. COX PH MODEL: Models the hazard h(t) = h₀(t) · exp(β₁X₁ + β₂X₂ + …). The baseline hazard h₀(t) is left unspecified (that's the 'semi-parametric' part). The exp(β) coefficients are hazard ratios — a Paydex drop of 20 points multiplying default hazard by 1.4 is immediately interpretable. The proportional hazards assumption (covariates shift the hazard by a constant multiplier over time) must be tested via Schoenfeld residuals. 5. PARAMETRIC & ML EXTENSIONS: Accelerated Failure Time (AFT) models (Weibull, log-normal) are fully parametric and produce median time-to-event predictions. Survival random forests (ranger, scikit-survival) and DeepSurv handle non-linear covariate interactions without the PH assumption. Competing-risks models handle multiple mutually exclusive outcomes (e.g. a firm can churn, go bankrupt, or get acquired). 6. EVALUATION: Concordance index (C-index) — analogous to AUC — measures how well the model rank-orders risk. Brier score measures calibration of predicted survival curves over time.

When To Use

Use survival analysis whenever the TIMING of an event matters, not just whether it happens. Red flags that standard classification is the wrong tool: your dataset has censored observations; you want to predict 'when' not just 'will'; you need to compare risk curves across segments; or your outcome window is variable across customers. In B2B data specifically: customer churn modelling (when will this account lapse?), credit risk (when will this firm first miss a payment?), sales velocity (how long does a deal of this type take to close?), and supplier risk (time-to-financial-distress given trade payment trends).

🎯 Worked Example — D&B Context

D&B PAYMENT DEFAULT EARLY-WARNING MODEL. Problem: A commercial bank using D&B data wants to know not just IF a small business will default on a trade payable in the next 12 months, but WHEN — so relationship managers can intervene at the right moment, not too early (false alarm fatigue) and not too late (loss already baked in). Data: 2M DUNS-linked firms, 36 months of monthly Paydex scores, Days Beyond Terms (DBT) trends, corporate family tree depth, industry NAICS, firmographic size band, and a binary flag for first 90-day delinquency event with the calendar month it occurred. Approach: (1) Fit Kaplan-Meier curves stratified by Paydex band (<50, 50-79, ≥80) — the visual immediately shows firms below Paydex 50 have a median time-to-default of 14 months vs. never crossing 50% default probability for the ≥80 cohort over the 36-month window. (2) Fit a Cox PH model with covariates: current Paydex, 3-month Paydex velocity (slope), DBT trend, industry default base rate, subsidiary vs. HQ flag. Hazard ratios reveal: a 10-point Paydex drop in a single month multiplies default hazard by 1.62 (HR=1.62, 95% CI 1.54–1.71) — a number a credit officer can act on and explain to a regulator. (3) Schoenfeld residual test flags that the HQ/subsidiary covariate violates PH (its effect changes over time), so it's modelled as a time-varying covariate. (4) DeepSurv is layered on for the non-linear interaction between Paydex velocity and industry sector, lifting the C-index from 0.74 to 0.81. Output fed into D&B's Pipeline Risk & Revenue Protection agent archetype: the agent surfaces accounts whose predicted median time-to-default falls within the next 90 days, ranked by expected exposure, triggering a relationship manager workflow in Salesforce Agentforce — with the Cox hazard ratios attached as the explainability layer so the rep understands exactly which signals drove the alert.
