🧠 Data Science Topic of the Day

🎓 Survival Analysis

Predict not just *if* something will happen — but *when*.

What It Is

Survival analysis is a family of statistical techniques originally developed in medical research to model time-to-event data — e.g., how long until a patient dies, a machine fails, or a customer churns. In B2B analytics, the 'event' can be anything with a clock attached: contract cancellation, late payment, account expansion, or company default. Unlike standard regression, survival models handle *censored* data gracefully — meaning records where the event hasn't happened yet (the customer is still alive at observation time) are not thrown away but correctly contribute partial information. The two workhorses are the Kaplan-Meier estimator (non-parametric survival curves) and the Cox Proportional Hazards model (semi-parametric regression that quantifies covariate effects on the hazard rate). Modern extensions include accelerated failure time (AFT) models and deep survival models like DeepHit and DeepSurv, which blend neural networks with survival objectives.

How It Works

1. DEFINE THE EVENT & TIME ORIGIN: e.g., event = customer churns, time origin = contract start date. Each observation has a duration T and a binary indicator delta (1 = event observed, 0 = censored). 2. KAPLAN-MEIER CURVES: Estimate S(t) = P(survive past time t) non-parametrically. No model assumptions. Great for exploratory comparisons between segments (e.g., SMB vs Enterprise). 3. LOG-RANK TEST: Statistically test whether two survival curves differ significantly — the survival-analysis equivalent of a t-test. 4. COX PROPORTIONAL HAZARDS: Models the hazard rate h(t|X) = h₀(t) × exp(β₁X₁ + β₂X₂ + …). The baseline hazard h₀(t) is left unspecified (semi-parametric). Coefficients exp(β) are *hazard ratios* — e.g., exp(β) = 1.4 for 'missed payment last quarter' means 40% higher instantaneous churn risk at any point in time. KEY ASSUMPTION: proportional hazards — the ratio of hazards between any two entities is constant over time. Violated? Use time-varying covariates or AFT models. 5. ACCELERATED FAILURE TIME (AFT): Instead of modelling the hazard, AFT directly models log(T) = β·X + ε. Easier to interpret: a coefficient of -0.3 means that covariate accelerates failure time by e^0.3 ≈ 35%. 6. DEEP SURVIVAL (DeepHit, DeepSurv): Replace the linear predictor in Cox with a neural network. Handle non-linear interactions and competing risks (e.g., a business can churn OR get acquired — two distinct terminal events). 7. OUTPUT: Predicted survival curves per entity S(t|Xᵢ), median survival time, and time-specific risk scores — all actionable.

When To Use

• CHURN PREDICTION WITH TIMING: Not just 'will they churn?' but 'they have a 70% chance of churning within 90 days' — enabling time-boxed interventions. • CREDIT & PAYMENT RISK: Predict days-to-default or days-to-late-payment given firmographic and payment history signals. Paydex scores are natural covariates. • ACCOUNT EXPANSION WINDOWS: Model time-to-upsell event — when does an account typically become receptive to a new product line? Feeds the Temporal Analytics / buying window concept directly. • EQUIPMENT / FLEET FAILURE: Predict asset failure timing for preventive maintenance (D&B Fleet & Asset agent archetype). • SALES CYCLE DURATION: Model how long deals stay in each pipeline stage — identify deals that have 'aged out' statistically and are unlikely to close. • VENDOR / SUPPLIER RISK: Predict time-to-insolvency for supply chain counterparties using financial distress signals.

🎯 Worked Example — D&B Context

SCENARIO — Pipeline Risk & Revenue Protection Agent at D&B. Problem: A large financial services client uses D&B data to monitor 4,000 active vendor relationships. Finance wants to know which vendors are at elevated risk of insolvency within the next 6 months — not just a binary risk flag, but a time-calibrated survival probability to prioritise contract renegotiation and contingency sourcing. DATA: Each vendor record has T = months since onboarding, delta = 1 if insolvency event confirmed in D&B's global business registry, 0 if still active. Covariates pulled from D&B's 600M+ record graph: Paydex score trajectory (3-month rolling delta), days-beyond-terms (DBT) trend, corporate family tree depth (subsidiary vs. parent), industry sector, geographic market stress index, and number of adverse legal filings. MODEL: Cox Proportional Hazards fit on 3 years of historical vendor cohorts. Key findings — hazard ratios: Paydex drop >15pts in 90 days → HR = 2.8 (most powerful predictor); DBT increasing trend → HR = 1.9; single-entity firm (no parent) → HR = 1.5; adverse legal filing → HR = 2.1. OUTPUT: For each of the 4,000 active vendors, the model scores S(180 days | Xᵢ) — survival probability at the 6-month horizon. Vendors below S(180) < 0.60 are flagged as Priority Red. The Pipeline Risk agent surfaces these weekly, attaches the Kaplan-Meier curve per vendor to the procurement dashboard, and triggers a workflow in ServiceNow to initiate alternative sourcing reviews. EXPLAINABILITY: Because Cox is semi-parametric and SHAP values can be computed over the linear predictor, each vendor flag arrives with a plain-language explanation: 'Vendor X flagged because Paydex dropped 22 points in Q2 and DBT has trended up for 3 consecutive months.' This is the Explainability First / Ground Truth principle in action — no black-box risk scores, just auditable reasoning grounded in proprietary D&B signals.
