🧠 Data Science Topic of the Day

🎓 Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)

Give your AI a filing cabinet: ground every generated answer in real, verifiable facts rather than baked-in model memory.

What It Is

RAG is an architecture that splits the job of 'knowing things' away from the job of 'reasoning and writing.' Instead of relying solely on facts frozen inside a model's weights at training time, RAG adds a live retrieval step: before generating a response, the system fetches the most relevant documents or data chunks from an external store — a vector database, a knowledge base, or a structured data lake — and hands them to the language model as context. The model then reasons over those retrieved facts to produce its answer. The result is a system that is factually grounded, auditable, and updatable without retraining.

How It Works

1. INDEXING: Source documents (PDFs, database rows, web pages, structured records) are chunked into passages and encoded into dense vector embeddings via an embedding model (e.g. OpenAI text-embedding-3, Cohere embed-v3). These vectors are stored in a vector database (Pinecone, Weaviate, pgvector). 2. RETRIEVAL: At query time the user's question is embedded using the same model. A nearest-neighbour search (approximate or exact) returns the top-k most semantically similar chunks — the 'retrieved context.' 3. AUGMENTATION: The retrieved chunks are injected into the prompt alongside the original question, forming a structured context window for the LLM. 4. GENERATION: The LLM (GPT-4o, Claude 3.5, Gemini 1.5) reads only what it was given and generates a grounded, cited answer. 5. ADVANCED PATTERNS: Hybrid retrieval (dense vector search + BM25 keyword search, re-ranked by a cross-encoder) dramatically improves precision. HyDE (Hypothetical Document Embeddings) embeds a hypothetical ideal answer to improve query representation. Agentic RAG adds a router or re-ranker agent that decides whether retrieval is even needed and can issue multiple sub-queries iteratively.

When To Use

Use RAG when: (a) facts change faster than you can retrain — product pricing, company financials, news; (b) you need source attribution and explainability — regulated industries, sales conversations; (c) your knowledge base is proprietary and must not leak into a shared model — D&B's 600M+ entity records; (d) the answer space is too large to fit in model weights — millions of company profiles; (e) hallucination is unacceptable — any downstream decision with real dollar consequences. Do NOT use RAG when: the question is purely analytical/mathematical (no documents help), latency budget is extremely tight with no caching, or your corpus is tiny enough to fit entirely in a single prompt.

🎯 Worked Example — D&B Context

Scenario — D&B Agentic Prospecting Agent. A sales rep types: 'Which mid-market manufacturers in the US Midwest added more than 50 employees in the last 6 months and have a Paydex score above 80?' Without RAG, a generic LLM invents plausible-sounding but fabricated company names. With RAG: Step 1 — the query is decomposed into filters (SIC codes 2000-3999, HQ states IL/OH/MI/WI/MN, employee growth delta, Paydex ≥ 80) and an embedding is generated. Step 2 — the retrieval layer hits D&B's indexed entity corpus, pulling the top-50 matching business record chunks ranked by semantic + structured hybrid search. Step 3 — those 50 real D&B records (DUNS, firmographic signals, Paydex, corporate family tree snippets) are injected into the prompt. Step 4 — the LLM synthesises a ranked prospect list with rationale, citing specific DUNS numbers and scores. The agent can then trigger the Account Intelligence archetype to enrich each result with intent signals from Buydex. Crucially, every claim in the output is traceable to a real D&B record — satisfying D&B's 'Explainability First / Ground Truth' strategic mandate and making the agent's reasoning auditable by the rep.
