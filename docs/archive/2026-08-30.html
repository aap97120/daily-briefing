🧠 Data Science Topic of the Day

🎓 Knowledge Graphs

Turning connected facts into structured intelligence — the reasoning layer that makes entity resolution actually mean something.

What It Is

A knowledge graph is a structured network of real-world entities (companies, people, products, locations) and the typed relationships between them, stored as subject–predicate–object triples (e.g., 'Acme Corp' → 'subsidiary_of' → 'GlobalHoldings Inc'). Unlike flat tables or document stores, a knowledge graph captures multi-hop context: you can traverse from a company to its parent, to that parent's other subsidiaries, to their shared ultimate beneficial owner, and back — all in a single query. The underlying data model is typically a property graph (Neo4j, Amazon Neptune) or an RDF triplestore (Apache Jena, Stardog). Knowledge graphs power entity disambiguation, corporate family tree navigation, supply chain risk mapping, and — critically for agentic AI — give LLM agents a grounded, queryable world model to reason over rather than relying purely on parametric memory.

How It Works

1. **Entity extraction & resolution** — Named Entity Recognition (NER) pulls entities from raw text, structured records, or APIs. A resolver (e.g., DUNS matching) canonicalises them to a single node ID, collapsing 'IBM Corp', 'International Business Machines', and 'IBM UK Ltd' into one authoritative entity. 2. **Relationship extraction** — Supervised classifiers or LLM prompts label the edge type between two entities (owns, supplies_to, competes_with, employs). Each edge carries attributes: confidence score, source provenance, valid-from/to dates. 3. **Graph storage & indexing** — Nodes and edges are persisted in a graph database. Property graphs use adjacency lists optimised for traversal; RDF stores use SPARQL for semantic reasoning. Vector embeddings (node2vec, TransE, RotatE) are often added alongside so that graph proximity aligns with embedding similarity. 4. **Query & traversal** — Cypher (Neo4j) or SPARQL retrieves multi-hop paths. Example: find all tier-2 suppliers of a target account that share an ultimate parent with a company flagged for sanctions. 5. **Inference & link prediction** — Graph neural networks (GNNs) like GraphSAGE or relational GCNs predict missing edges — e.g., an unlabelled 'likely competitor' relationship — by aggregating neighbourhood features. 6. **Agentic integration** — An LLM agent calls the graph as a tool (via MCP or LangChain tool-calling), asking structured questions and getting back grounded, citable facts rather than hallucinated ones.

When To Use

Use a knowledge graph when: (a) your data is inherently relational and multi-hop paths matter (corporate hierarchies, supply chains, fraud rings); (b) entity disambiguation is load-bearing — you need to know that two differently-named records are the same real-world thing; (c) you need explainable, auditable reasoning chains rather than black-box similarity scores; (d) you are building agentic systems that need a grounded world model to prevent hallucination; (e) your analytical questions involve 'degrees of separation' (who is two hops from this sanctioned entity?). Avoid over-engineering toward a graph when your data is truly tabular and relationships are shallow — a well-joined relational schema is simpler and faster in those cases.

**D&B Account Expansion Agent — 'WhiteSpace Navigator'** Challenge: A D&B enterprise customer (a global bank) wants to identify upsell opportunities across its 4,000-account portfolio. They already buy D&B's Finance & Risk suite but have zero penetration of the Sales & Marketing products. Graph construction: D&B's Knowledge Graph ingests three layers — (1) Corporate family trees from the D&B entity graph (DUNS-resolved, 600M+ records), linking every account to its domestic and global ultimate parents and all siblings. (2) Product ownership edges: which DUNS numbers have active D&B contracts and for which product lines. (3) Firmographic/signal nodes: Paydex scores, Buydex intent signals, headcount growth events, and M&A activity, all timestamped as edge attributes. Query at runtime: The Account Expansion Agent receives a prompt — 'Find subsidiaries of our current Finance-only customers that (a) have shown intent for sales intelligence products in the last 90 days, (b) have grown headcount >15% YoY, and (c) are NOT already D&B Sales & Marketing customers.' This translates into a three-hop Cypher query across the graph: Customer node → [subsidiary_of]⁻¹ → Sibling entities → filter on Buydex intent edge (last 90 days) → filter on headcount growth node attribute → anti-join on existing contract edges. Output: The graph returns 47 DUNS-resolved entities with full provenance: which parent they roll up to, what intent signals fired, and the confidence score on each relationship. The agent then ranks by Paydex-weighted revenue potential and drafts a personalised outreach sequence for each — grounded in verifiable graph facts, not LLM guesswork. Why this matters for D&B: The DUNS entity resolution layer IS the graph's foundation. No competitor can replicate the 600M-node starting point or the depth of corporate family trees.
