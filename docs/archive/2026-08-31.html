🧠 Data Science Topic of the Day

🎓 Multimodal Models

Teaching AI to read, see, and reason across data types simultaneously — so a single model can ingest a PDF, a chart, a table, and a news headline and produce one coherent answer.

What It Is

Multimodal models are neural networks trained on more than one data modality at once — typically combinations of text, images, structured tables, audio, or video. Unlike a language model that only reads text, a multimodal model encodes each modality into a shared representational space (a joint embedding), then reasons across all of them together. GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro, and Claude 3 are the most widely deployed examples. The key insight is that real-world business signals don't arrive in a single format: a company's creditworthiness lives in its filed PDF accounts, its satellite-visible parking lot activity, its scanned trade-credit invoices, and its news mentions — all at once.

How It Works

1. ENCODING: Each modality gets its own encoder — a vision transformer (ViT) for images, a standard transformer for text, a graph encoder for structured relationships. Each encoder maps its input into a fixed-dimensional vector. 2. ALIGNMENT / FUSION: A cross-attention or projection layer maps all modality vectors into a single shared latent space. Contrastive pre-training (like CLIP for image-text pairs) forces representations of the same concept across modalities to be close together. 3. JOINT REASONING: A large language model decoder (or a unified transformer) attends over the fused multimodal context and generates or classifies. 4. FINE-TUNING: Task-specific heads or LoRA adapters are added on top for downstream tasks (classification, extraction, Q&A). The critical engineering challenge is modality alignment — making sure 'a photo of a warehouse' and 'the text description of that warehouse' land in the same neighbourhood of the embedding space.

When To Use

Use multimodal models when: (a) your signal lives across formats and stitching them manually loses fidelity — e.g. extracting financials from scanned annual reports alongside textual risk commentary; (b) you want document-level intelligence without brittle OCR pipelines — ingest the PDF as-is; (c) you are building agents that must reason over web screenshots, dashboards, or satellite/street-view imagery alongside structured company data; (d) you need few-shot generalisation across new company types without full retraining. Do NOT reach for multimodal when all your data is already clean structured text — a standard LLM or classifier is cheaper and faster.

🎯 Worked Example — D&B Context

D&B SUPPLIER RISK SCREEN — MULTIMODAL EDITION. Problem: A Fortune 500 procurement team needs to assess 3,000 supplier sites annually. Structured D&B signals (Paydex, DUNS family tree, firmographics) capture financial health, but miss physical operational risk — is the factory actually running? Classic pipeline: analyst manually checks Google Maps, scans PDF inspection reports, reads news. Slow, inconsistent, unscalable. Multimodal solution: Step 1 — For each DUNS-resolved supplier, pull three inputs: (a) the most recent annual report PDF (text + embedded tables + charts), (b) a satellite image of the primary facility address (sourced from a commercial imagery API), (c) the last 90 days of D&B news mentions as text. Step 2 — A multimodal model (e.g. Gemini 1.5 Pro via API, or a fine-tuned InternVL2 on-prem) encodes all three into a joint embedding per supplier. Step 3 — A lightweight classifier head trained on historical disruption labels predicts a 'Physical Operational Risk' score (0–100). Step 4 — The score is written back to the D&B supplier profile and surfaced in the Account Intelligence agent archetype. Business output: analysts review only the top-decile flagged suppliers rather than all 3,000. The satellite channel catches dark facilities (parking lots empty, loading bays idle) 6–8 weeks before a payment delinquency shows up in Paydex — a genuine temporal leading indicator. D&B's DUNS entity resolution is the glue: it ensures the PDF, the imagery coordinates, and the news mentions are all pinned to the same legal entity, not fuzzy name matches.
