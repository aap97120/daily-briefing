🧠 Data Science Topic of the Day

🎓 Bayesian Inference

Stop asking 'is this statistically significant?' Start asking 'what should I believe now?'

What It Is

Bayesian inference is a framework for updating beliefs in the light of new evidence. Instead of producing a single point estimate ('the conversion rate is 4.2%'), it produces a full probability distribution over possible values ('here is how likely each conversion rate is, given everything we have observed'). The core engine is Bayes' Theorem: Posterior ∝ Likelihood × Prior. Your Prior encodes what you believed before seeing data. The Likelihood measures how well each hypothesis explains the data you actually saw. The Posterior is the updated belief you carry forward — and becomes the Prior for the next update. This iterative, cumulative learning is what makes Bayesian methods feel like reasoning rather than testing.

How It Works

1. DEFINE A PRIOR — Express your pre-existing belief as a probability distribution. For a conversion rate you might use a Beta(2, 20) prior, encoding a belief that rates are probably low but not zero. For a revenue forecast you might use a Normal distribution centred on last quarter's result. Priors can be 'weakly informative' (broad, humble) or 'informative' (tight, confident). 2. COLLECT DATA & WRITE THE LIKELIHOOD — Choose a generative model: Binomial for conversion counts, Poisson for event counts, Gaussian for continuous outcomes. The likelihood function scores every possible parameter value by how probable the observed data would be under that value. 3. COMPUTE THE POSTERIOR — Multiply prior by likelihood at every parameter value and normalise. For conjugate pairs (Beta-Binomial, Gamma-Poisson, Normal-Normal) this is analytic and instant. For complex models use Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) via Stan or PyMC, or Variational Inference (faster, approximate) via NumPyro or TensorFlow Probability. 4. MAKE DECISIONS FROM THE POSTERIOR — Report the posterior mean as your best estimate, the 95% Credible Interval as your uncertainty range, and the probability that a parameter exceeds a threshold (e.g., P(lift > 0) = 87%) as your actionable decision signal. Unlike a p-value, this number means exactly what it sounds like.

When To Use

USE IT WHEN: (a) You have small samples and cannot wait for a classical A/B test to reach significance — Bayesian methods extract signal immediately and quantify uncertainty honestly rather than forcing a binary reject/fail-to-reject. (b) You want to incorporate historical knowledge — prior quarters' data, industry benchmarks, or expert judgment — rather than pretending every experiment starts from scratch. (c) You need continuous monitoring without inflating Type I error — Bayesian posteriors are valid to inspect at any time; no multiple-comparison penalty. (d) You are building a probabilistic model that feeds downstream decisions — a posterior distribution is a richer input to a decision engine than a single coefficient. AVOID IT WHEN: prior specification is politically contentious, your audience demands a familiar p-value, or you need sub-second latency at massive scale (MCMC is slow; use VI or conjugate shortcuts instead).

🎯 Worked Example — D&B Context

PROBLEM — D&B's Prospecting Agent emails a new outbound sequence to 120 mid-market CFOs promoting Finance Analytics. After one week, 9 have clicked (7.5%). The team asks: is this good enough to scale, or should we iterate? With n=120 a classical test has almost no power. BAYESIAN APPROACH — Step 1: Prior. Historical D&B outbound click rates for this segment cluster around 5–9%. Encode as Beta(6, 90) — mean 6.3%, modest confidence. Step 2: Likelihood. 9 clicks from 120 emails ~ Binomial(120, θ). Step 3: Posterior. Beta(6+9, 90+111) = Beta(15, 201). Posterior mean = 15/216 = 6.9%. 95% Credible Interval: [4.0%, 11.1%]. Step 4: Decision. P(θ > 8%) = 22% — not enough evidence to scale confidently. P(θ > 5%) = 91% — strong evidence the sequence is beating the floor threshold. VERDICT — keep sequence live, run 2 more weeks of data, re-update posterior. No arbitrary significance gate, no wasted experiment restart. The posterior from week 1 becomes the prior for week 2, compounding learning. EXTENSION — layer in Buydex intent scores as a covariate in a hierarchical Bayesian model: accounts with high Buydex prior-week get their own θ stratum, letting the model borrow strength across segments while respecting differences. This is exactly the kind of temporal, entity-resolved signal that makes D&B data uniquely powerful in a Bayesian workflow.
